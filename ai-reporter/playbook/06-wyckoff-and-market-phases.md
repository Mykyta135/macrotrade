# Wyckoff Method & Market Phases
Source: Voluma Trading Wyckoff Course + Order Flow Guide

## Core Principle
Markets move through four repeating phases: Accumulation → Uptrend → Distribution → Downtrend. Understanding which phase a pair is in radically changes how you interpret signals. A system BUY during a Distribution phase is a trap. A system SELL during an Accumulation phase is a trap. Wyckoff teaches us to follow the footprints of large, well-informed traders rather than trade against them.

---

## Rules

### 1. The four Wyckoff market phases
- **Accumulation**: A sideways range where large institutions quietly buy while retail traders are bearish. Characterized by a series of equal lows or slightly declining lows that keep getting bought. Volume increases on upside moves within the range.
- **Uptrend (Markup)**: Price breaks out of accumulation with conviction. Higher highs and higher lows. Retail traders begin to pile in long near the end of this phase.
- **Distribution**: A sideways range where large institutions quietly sell while retail traders are bullish (buying the highs). Characterized by equal highs that keep getting rejected. Volume increases on downside moves within the range.
- **Downtrend (Markdown)**: Price breaks out of distribution to the downside. Lower lows and lower highs.
- **Re-accumulation**: A pause/pullback within an uptrend where institutions add to longs before the next leg up.
- **Re-distribution**: A pause/rally within a downtrend where institutions add to shorts before the next leg down.

### 2. Identify the current phase before evaluating any signal
- If a pair is in a clear **Accumulation** range (repeated equal lows, increasing volume on up-moves): BUY bias appropriate when breakout confirms, but not before. A SELL signal here is very low probability.
- If a pair is in a **Distribution** range (repeated equal highs, increasing volume on down-moves): SELL bias appropriate when breakout confirms. A BUY signal here is a trap.
- If a pair is in a markup **Uptrend** with re-accumulations: BUY setups on pullbacks to re-accumulation zones are high probability.
- If a pair is in a markdown **Downtrend** with re-distributions: SELL setups on rallies to re-distribution zones are high probability.

### 3. Volume confirms the phase
- Accumulation: volume should increase on bullish moves within the range, decrease on bearish moves
- Distribution: volume should increase on bearish moves within the range, decrease on bullish moves
- During markup (uptrend): volume should be higher on the up legs than the down legs
- If volume tells the opposite story → the phase you think you're in may be wrong

### 4. Path of least resistance
- When there is insufficient Supply in the way of buyers (accumulation complete), the path of least resistance is UP
- When there is insufficient Demand in the way of sellers (distribution complete), the path of least resistance is DOWN
- Always ask: has the supply/demand been cleared? Is the path clear?
- Application: A BUY signal is higher probability if the pair has recently cleared a major supply zone (no overhead supply). A SELL signal is higher probability if the pair has recently cleared a major demand zone.

### 5. Supply absorption = institutional buying
- In a downtrend or at a low, if price enters a demand area and the candles show: price dips into the zone, large volume trades, but price barely moves down → sellers are being absorbed by buyers
- This is a sign of institutional accumulation: they are matching every sell order with a buy order, preventing price from falling further
- The result is eventually a reversal once all the sell orders at that level are absorbed
- Application: A pair that has been in a prolonged downtrend but has recently shown low ATR (price not moving much despite attempts) near a major demand zone → likely accumulation. Apply WATCH or upgrade system SELL to MODIFIED.

### 6. Common Wyckoff reversal signals
- **Spring**: Price dips below a key support level (sweeping stops below equal lows), then reverses sharply back above it. This is a bullish signal — the dip below was a stop hunt to clear sellers before the uptrend begins. Classic accumulation completion signal.
- **Upthrust After Distribution (UTAD)**: Price pushes above a key resistance level (sweeping stops above equal highs), then reverses sharply below it. This is a bearish signal — the push above was a stop hunt to clear buyers before the downtrend begins. Classic distribution completion signal.
- Application: When headlines describe a surprise break of a key level that immediately reverses — this is likely a Spring or UTAD. Align with the post-reversal direction, not the initial breakout.

### 7. Cause and effect principle
- The size of the trend (effect) is proportional to the time spent in accumulation/distribution (cause)
- A pair that spent 3 months ranging (accumulating) will have a larger uptrend than one that accumulated for 3 weeks
- Application: A signal on a pair with a long-standing range (potential multi-month accumulation) has higher potential reward than one that has only just started forming a base.

### 8. Effort vs result (volume confirmation)
- A large-volume candle that moves price significantly = effort + result alignment → trend is confirmed, likely to continue
- A large-volume candle that barely moves price = effort without result → the move is being absorbed by the opposite side, potential reversal ahead
- Application: If a major news event caused a large volume spike but price barely moved → this is institutional absorption. The next move may be against the news direction.

## How to apply to MacroTrade pairs

- Before confirming any system direction, evaluate what phase the pair is currently in on the weekly/daily chart.
- If system says SELL but pair is completing a Spring (bullish reversal below key support) → aiVerdict REJECTED, adjust -2
- If system says BUY but pair is completing a UTAD (bearish reversal above key resistance) → aiVerdict REJECTED, adjust -2
- If system direction aligns with the Wyckoff phase AND the market structure is confirming → aiConfidenceAdjustment +1 or +2
- Pairs in genuine distribution (not just a simple consolidation) should be treated as sell candidates even if fundamental data appears mixed
- Pairs in genuine accumulation (not just a simple consolidation) should be treated as buy candidates even if recent momentum appears weak
