# Sessions, Timing & Killzone Rules
Source: jeaFX Bootcamp (Episodes 10, 11) + Institutional Knowledge

## Core Principle
Forex market sessions are not just time windows — they represent different pools of institutional capital from different geographies becoming active. Each session has characteristic behavior driven by which institutions are awake and trading. Understanding session dynamics helps avoid false moves and identify the highest-probability timing for entries.

---

## Rules

### 1. Three core sessions and their primary currencies
- **Asia Session** (1:00 AM – 7:00 AM UK time / 8:00 PM – 2:00 AM EST): AUD, NZD, JPY see highest volume. EUR, GBP, CHF, USD see lower volume and thinner, less reliable moves.
- **London Session** (8:00 AM – 12:00 PM UK time / 3:00 AM – 7:00 AM EST): GBP, EUR, CHF see highest volume. This is the most volatile session for European pairs.
- **New York Session** (1:00 PM – 5:00 PM UK time / 8:00 AM – 12:00 PM EST): USD, CAD see highest volume. The London/NY overlap (8AM-12PM EST) is the highest volume window of the entire trading day.

### 2. London/NY overlap is the premier trading window
- The hour window where London and New York sessions overlap (approximately 8AM-12PM EST) produces the highest volume, sharpest moves, and most reliable setups
- This is when institutional players from both the European and American markets are simultaneously active
- Major directional moves for GBP, EUR, USD pairs frequently begin or accelerate in this overlap window
- Application: Signals that were set up overnight in Asia but have not yet moved should be evaluated in the context of whether London or NY session will confirm or invalidate the setup

### 3. Asia session = range building, not trend initiating
- Asia session typically creates a range with relatively quiet conditions (unless major Asian news or events)
- This range often acts as a liquidity pool: London session will frequently sweep the Asia high or Asia low before establishing the real direction
- The Asia range high = buy-stop cluster (potential sweep and reversal point)
- The Asia range low = sell-stop cluster (potential sweep and reversal point)
- Do not take the Asia breakout trade directly — wait to see if London sweeps the range and reverses

### 4. London session often reverses the previous day's Asia direction
- London institutions frequently open by taking liquidity from Asia session extremes
- A common pattern: Asia pushes a pair lower → London opens and sweeps those lows → then rallies strongly for the session
- Application: An overnight downtrend in AUD/USD during Asia does not necessarily signal continued downside in London. Evaluate fresh at London open.

### 5. Session-based killzones for entry timing
The highest-quality entries cluster in specific time windows:
- **London open (8-9AM UK time)**: initial session direction often established here, can be volatile
- **London AM killzone (9-11AM UK time)**: highest probability window for London session setups
- **New York open (1-2PM UK time / 8-9AM EST)**: often creates the daily high or low, significant reversals and breakouts
- **NY AM killzone (2-4PM UK time / 9-11AM EST)**: prime window for NY setups
- Outside these windows, price action is lower quality with more noise

### 6. Avoid trading in the dead zone
- The period between the end of London (12PM UK) and the start of NY (1PM UK) is the "lunch lull" — thin volume, erratic moves, no institutional activity
- After NY closes (5PM UK onward into Asia open), the same thin-market conditions apply
- Application: System signals generated from data captured in thin-market hours should be treated with more skepticism. Mark as WATCH if the signal coincides with a dead zone period.

### 7. Day of week seasonality
- **Monday**: often slow, directional bias being established. Lower confidence signals.
- **Tuesday/Wednesday**: highest volatility, most institutional activity. Highest confidence signals.
- **Thursday**: can be volatile around US data releases, ECB/BOE decisions. Watch calendar.
- **Friday**: position squaring before weekend. Moves can reverse into close. Reduce confidence on late-Friday signals, especially for pairs with open weekend risk.
- Application: If today is Friday afternoon, reduce confidence on any new BUY or SELL signal by -1. Do not initiate new trades into a weekend with open geopolitical risk.

### 8. Major economic event windows
- 30 minutes before and after high-impact news (NFP, CPI, FOMC, ECB/BOE decisions) = danger zone
- Liquidity is thin before the event (everyone waiting) and volatile after
- Spreads widen, stop hunts are common, and the initial post-news spike is frequently reversed
- Application: If the MacroTrade danger zone is active (high-impact events within 24H), apply WATCH to all signals regardless of system grade. Wait for initial volatility to settle before assessing direction.

### 9. London gap fill
- When the forex market opens Monday morning in Asia after a weekend, there is often a gap from the Friday close
- This gap is frequently (not always) filled during the early part of the week
- Application: A gap-down open in EUR/USD at Monday Asia open does not guarantee a continued selloff — London may fill the gap first. Mark Monday Asia open signals as WATCH until London session direction is established.

---

## How to apply to MacroTrade pairs

- For AUD/USD and NZD/USD: the key driver sessions are Asia (initial setup) and London/NY (confirmation and continuation). Sweeps of Asia range in London are very common.
- For EUR/USD and GBP/USD: London open and NY open are the primary signal windows. Asia moves in these pairs are often noise.
- For USD/JPY: driven by both Asia (JPY side) and NY (USD side). BOJ intervention risk is highest during thin Tokyo liquidity windows.
- For GBP/JPY: highest volatility pair, moves heavily in both London and Tokyo. Extreme caution during any single-session analysis.
- Current session context should inform whether a signal is "ready to execute now" (setup in active session) vs "needs to wait" (good setup but wrong session window).
